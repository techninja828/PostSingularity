- Sources: `S2`

### Generative genome-design success rates can be inflated by selection and manufacturing filters

- Event date: 2026-11-20
- Sources: `S7`
- Observed fact: A BioSystems paper published on 2026-11-20 argues that reported success rates for generative bacteriophage-genome design can conflate generator performance with downstream computational filtering, sequence recovery, and biological viability. Its simulations found that phenotype-informed selection could inflate apparent success rates from 0.58 to between 0.63 and 0.91, while selection-conditioned pipeline responses differed from the generator’s intrinsic response.
- Significance: This directly challenges optimistic interpretations of AI-assisted synthetic biology benchmarks. The result strengthens the case for prospective, stratified experiments that retain failures and separately measure sequence novelty, technical recovery, and biological phenotype.
