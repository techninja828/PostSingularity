### A rare functional MSTN variant is associated with increased human lean mass

- Event date: 2026-11-20
- Sources: `S2`
- Observed fact: A genome-wide association study published on 2026-11-20 analyzed lean mass measured by dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry and identified 63 associated loci. The strongest reported signal was a rare missense variant in MSTN, p.Ile225Thr, with an effect of beta = 0.28 standard deviations for leg lean mass, a 95% confidence interval of 0.19 to 0.37, and P = 1.9 × 10^-9. Functional analysis supported a role for the variant in skeletal-muscle biology.
- Significance: The finding provides human genetic evidence that naturally occurring variation in the myostatin pathway can influence muscle mass. It strengthens the rationale for therapies aimed at preserving or increasing lean mass in disease and aging, and supplies a measurable biological reference point for future enhancement-oriented interventions. It does not establish safe, controllable enhancement in healthy people. Limitations: The association involves a rare variant and does not show that reproducing the variant pharmacologically or through gene editing is safe. Lean mass is not equivalent to strength, endurance, healthspan, or functional performance. The study is not a clinical intervention and provides no evidence about delivery, dosage, reversibility, or long-term adverse effects. Potential pleiotropic effects and population transferability require further study.
