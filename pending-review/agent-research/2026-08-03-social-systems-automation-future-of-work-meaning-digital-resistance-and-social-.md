- Event date: 2026-07-28

- Event date: 2026-07-14
- Sources: `S13`, `S14`
- Observed fact: A systematic review published on 2026-06-23 reported that implantable brain-computer interfaces require recalibration because neural signals are inherently unstable. The study concerned restoration of localized hand sensation, not emotional communication or general brain-to-AI exchange.
- Significance: These findings narrow PS-NEURO-001. Neural interfaces are producing meaningful clinical progress, but durable, high-bandwidth, bidirectional sensory or affective communication remains unsupported by the available evidence. Recalibration, electrode attrition, surgery, maintenance, and clinical dependence are deployment constraints that make universal neural integration a much stronger claim than current results justify.

### PS-NEURO-001: High-bandwidth neural interfaces connect people and AI

- Proposed verdict: **weakened**
- Confidence: **high**
- Sources: `S13`, `S14`, `S15`
- Evidence: Clinical neural interfaces are producing useful localized sensory restoration, including multi-year stimulation, but current evidence also shows signal instability, recalibration requirements, electrode attrition, surgery and maintenance constraints, and small clinical cohorts. No material evidence supports high-bandwidth bidirectional communication carrying rich sensory or emotional information between people and AI.
- Real-world implication: Neural interfaces remain promising medical technologies with constrained capabilities, not general-purpose communication systems. Privacy, security, access, ethical classification, and long-term reliability are substantial barriers to consumer-scale augmentation.
- PostSingularity implication: Rich neural-to-AI communication remains a possible long-term capability rather than an established premise. A post-singularity setting using it should account for maintenance, calibration, neural-data control, unequal access, failure modes, and the distinction between localized clinical interfaces and full affective integration.

### PS-SPACE-001: AI and abundant energy enable sustained off-world settlement

- Proposed verdict: **weakened**
- Confidence: **high**
- Sources: `S15`, `S16`, `S17`
- Evidence: Current evidence remains centered on terrestrial analog missions, incremental validation, regular resupply, manual life-support servicing, and continuous biomedical monitoring. No material evidence in the priority window demonstrates reliable closed-loop life support, autonomous human-habitat operations, in-space manufacturing at settlement scale, improved settlement economics, or civilian communities operating with limited Earth support.
- Real-world implication: AI, autonomy, propulsion, and life-support advances may improve mission feasibility, but sustained off-world settlement is not yet demonstrated as a practical closed-loop system. Human health, maintenance, logistics, reliability, and economic constraints remain decisive.
- PostSingularity implication: A post-singularity civilization may eventually support durable orbital or off-world communities, but abundance and AI capability do not by themselves remove biological, ecological, governance, and maintenance constraints. Early settlements would likely remain dependent on robust human oversight and external infrastructure until closure and reliability are proven.
