## Executive Summary

During 2026-10-06 through 2026-10-13, AI accountability mechanisms became more formal through enforcement channels, proposed independent audits, lifecycle standards, public hearings, critical-infrastructure scrutiny, and participatory consultations. The evidence strengthens the case for layered provenance and oversight, but does not show that labels, logs, audits, or complaints establish authenticity, safety, accuracy, or public trust. Evidence on collective intelligence is mixed: AI-mediated deliberation can increase perceived trust while steering outcomes without improving consensus or participation equity, algorithmic decisions can reduce legitimacy in punitive or individualized contexts, and participation can leave decision rights concentrated. PS-AI-003 and PS-GOV-001 are therefore mixed; PS-NEURO-001, PS-NEURO-002, and PS-SPACE-001 remain insufficient-evidence. The repository mapper recommends targeted revisions to Trust Fabrics, AI Trust, and Governance Systems while preserving the registry and canon unchanged for human review.
