### AI assistance can improve immediate output while weakening the development of junior professional judgment

- Event date: 2026-10-07 publication of findings; three-month field experiment
- Sources: `S11`, `S12`
- Observed fact: A three-month randomized field experiment involving 133 patent lawyers at eleven intellectual-property law firms found that AI assistance improved drafting quality and reduced task time. However, when participants later completed a judgment task without AI, senior lawyers who had used AI showed stronger judgment while junior lawyers showed no average skill gain, with their results splitting into more very strong and more very weak scores. The researchers identified on-the-job learning as a potential casualty when AI removes routine but formative work.
- Significance: This narrows optimistic claims that automation naturally liberates people for more meaningful or higher-level contribution. If routine work is also the pathway through which novices acquire judgment, automating it may produce a thinner pipeline of experienced workers, greater dependence on existing experts, and more unequal access to status and meaningful contribution. It is a counterexample to the assumption that removing difficult or tedious work automatically improves human development. Limitations: The study concerns patent drafting and professional legal work, so generalization to other occupations is uncertain. The experiment was conducted with firms having regular, non-exclusive business relationships with Google, which may affect technology exposure or organizational behavior. The study measured professional judgment over a three-month period and does not establish long-term career, wage, or employment effects. The finding that junior outcomes split into stronger and weaker results does not prove that AI always harms novice learning; training design and supervision may moderate the effect.

The estimated value pool is not equivalent to worker income, public abundance, or reductions in working time. ### Provenance requirements can create audit trails without establishing truth, authenticity, or complete coverage

- Sources: `S2`, `S17`, `S18`

- Significance: This narrows PS-AI-003. Stronger provenance and audit systems are being institutionalized, but their social value is conditional. Limitations: The EU code creates a framework for compliance but does not provide measured adoption, coverage, detection accuracy, or public-trust outcomes. C2PA provenance is not an authenticity guarantee and cannot solve all forms of deception or misleading framing. The Berkeley source is a technical research record rather than a deployment study during the priority window. The evidence does not establish that provenance systems will fail; it shows that their scope is narrower than broad claims about social verification might imply.

- Evidence is stronger for institutional and English-language developments than for informal communities, local labor practices, non-English cultural responses, or analog participation.

- No direct evidence was supplied for neural-interface performance, long-term implant safety, or human-AI sensory and emotional communication.
- Space-related evidence concerns announcements and planned infrastructure rather than completed systems, sustained settlement, or closed-loop habitat performance.
- Provenance adoption, coverage, detection accuracy, privacy effects, and measurable effects on public trust remain uncertain.
- It remains uncertain how AI productivity gains will be distributed among employers, workers, governments, and communities.
- The evidence packets differ in emphasis on the meaning of AI productivity: the first packet reports limited large-scale displacement and modest reported time savings, while the counterevidence packet reports that organizations are using those gains to expand output, broaden job scopes, and preserve or increase organizational value rather than reduce compulsory work.

## Watchlist

- Measured changes in actual working hours, overtime, job scope, and time allocated to care, civic, creative, or relational activity.
- Basic-income, shorter-workweek, or other mechanisms that redistribute automation gains rather than merely increasing output.
- Longitudinal evidence on junior-worker skill formation, access to professional status, wages, and entry-level employment under AI assistance.
- Deployment rates, interoperability, robustness, privacy performance, and public-trust effects of provenance and labeling systems.
- Policy adoption concerning human accountability, worker voice, automated hiring, scheduling, monitoring, and dismissal decisions.
- Behavioral evidence for device-free spaces, analog-media use, low-technology communities, and intergenerational digital refusal.
- Neural-interface milestones including durable bidirectional implants, channel capacity, tissue response, decoded speech or affect, and privacy safeguards.
- Launch costs, mission duration, life-support closure, in-space manufacturing, autonomous maintenance, radiation protection, and human-health outcomes for off-world systems.
- Completion and performance of announced AI-enabled science, nuclear-space, lunar, and Mars infrastructure projects.
