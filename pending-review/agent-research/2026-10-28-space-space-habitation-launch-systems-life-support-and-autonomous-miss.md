### NASA's Psyche enters a sustained solar-electric-propulsion phase for deep-space transit

- Event date: 2026-10-22
- Sources: `S7`, `S8`
- Observed fact: NASA's Psyche spacecraft began using its solar-electric propulsion system nearly continuously on September 28, 2026, with only weekly pauses for engineering checks. - Significance: This is a concrete long-duration demonstration of high-efficiency propulsion: very low continuous thrust can accumulate into deep-space velocity while preserving propellant for years of operations. It strengthens the case for autonomous missions and solar-powered logistics beyond Earth orbit, though it is not evidence that electric propulsion can replace high-thrust systems for launch, landing, or rapid crew transport.

### Martian dust measurements identify fine and ultrafine particles relevant to habitat and life-support design

- Event date: 2026-10-26
- Sources: `S9`, `S10`
- Observed fact: NASA reported on October 26 that Perseverance's PIXL and SHERLOC instruments measured Martian dust collected on spacesuit-material samples over more than 1,645 Martian days. ### The Swift rescue attempt failed to preserve an aging spacecraft, exposing limits of autonomous orbital servicing

- Event date: 2026-10-23
- Sources: `S16`, `S17`
- Observed fact: NASA reported on October 23 that the Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory ended science operations after its orbit decayed rapidly. A high-risk servicing mission intended to boost the spacecraft did not succeed, and the observatory later entered safe mode at an altitude of approximately 275 kilometers before NASA ended operations in preparation for reentry.
- Significance: This is counterevidence to broad assumptions that autonomous or commercially provided in-space servicing can already make orbital infrastructure persistent and maintainable. The mission involved a valuable, mature spacecraft and a targeted rescue attempt, yet the servicing effort did not restore the spacecraft’s orbit. Orbital maintenance therefore remains sensitive to timing, atmospheric conditions, rendezvous capability, and the reliability of the servicing vehicle itself.
