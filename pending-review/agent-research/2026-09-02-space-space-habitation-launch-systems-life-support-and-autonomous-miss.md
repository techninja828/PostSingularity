- Event date: 2026-08-26
- Sources: `S1`
- Observed fact: On August 26, 2026, NASA reported the start of a new test phase inside a 60-foot spherical vacuum chamber to measure how lander engine plumes disturb lunar regolith. ## Watchlist

- Launch cadence, realized launch cost, vehicle reuse, and regulatory throughput across multiple missions rather than isolated successful flights.
- Flight-validated closed-loop life support, especially carbon-dioxide removal, consumables management, maintenance intervals, backup requirements, and performance in small spacecraft.
- Independent demonstrations of Earth-independent health care, Mars in-situ resource utilization, propellant production, and autonomous multi-agent coordination.
- Long-duration habitat demonstrations addressing micrometeoroids, radiation, thermal cycling, degradation, docking loads, and maintenance.
- Operational autonomous spacecraft results that quantify onboard decision-making versus ground supervision, navigation accuracy, recovery behavior, and failure rates.
- Successful autonomous rendezvous, capture, servicing, and orbital-boost demonstrations after the LINK attitude-control issue.
- Robot deployment counts, productive-hour economics, dexterous manipulation, transfer across tasks and environments, and adoption in transport, construction, maintenance, and care.
- AI transparency and provenance standards, audit requirements, regulatory disclosure rules, and measured adoption or resistance for high-impact systems.
- Neural-interface channel capacity, bidirectional sensory or affective decoding, long-term implant safety, and privacy protections.
- AI-assisted public deliberation, citizen assemblies, algorithmic impact review, temporary governance bodies, and evidence of legitimacy compared with fixed institutions.
- Concrete Artemis interoperability standards, compliance audits, dispute-resolution mechanisms, and coordination outcomes among lunar operators.
