## Executive Summary

The audited window shows genuine progress in battery storage, grid-forming capability, distributed resilience, and private-fusion infrastructure. A 50 MW grid-forming battery is operational in Scotland, a 150 MW/600 MWh battery is commercially operating in Virginia, U.S. storage additions reached 48.3 GWh in the first three quarters of 2026, and Puerto Rico’s virtual power plant serves nearly 115,000 households. These developments strengthen the case for storage and distributed systems as important energy infrastructure. They do not establish universal, cheap, reliable, or seasonally resilient energy abundance. Transmission and distribution bottlenecks, interconnection, permitting, financing, affordability, persistent outages, and short storage durations remain material constraints. Fusion progress is limited to intermediate plasma-temperature and licensing milestones, not net electricity or commercial operation. The evidence therefore qualifies PS-ENERGY-001 while providing insufficient evidence for the related space, AI-trust, neurotechnology, and social assumptions. - Event date: 2026-11-13
- Sources: `S1`, `S2`

### IEA’s World Energy Outlook 2026 quantifies both storage growth and the grid bottleneck

- Event date: 2026-11-19
- Sources: `S3`, `S4`
- Observed fact: The International Energy Agency published its World Energy Outlook 2026 on November 19, 2026. The report states that nearly 25 GW of utility-scale battery additions were paired with renewables in 2025, while total utility-scale battery additions rose by more than 55% to 93 GW. It reports that co-located renewable-plus-storage projects are expanding, but that they do not eliminate the need for wider network reinforcement. The report also estimates that grid-enhancing technologies could unlock 450–700 GW of additional hosting capacity, while flexible connections and related measures could enable a further 750–900 GW of demand to connect to existing grids. - Significance: This is the strongest broad quantitative signal in the window for the clean-energy-abundance thesis and its constraints. - Event date: 2026-11-17
- Sources: `S13`
