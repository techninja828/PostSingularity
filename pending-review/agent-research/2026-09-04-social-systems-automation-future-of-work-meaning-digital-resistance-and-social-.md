## Research Question

## Executive Summary

The audited evidence shows uneven social adaptation to automation and digital integration. AI-mediated hiring may narrow entry-level pathways and increase the value of referrals, networks, and demonstrated human judgment, while basic-income effects on work, well-being, and meaning remain untested in the supplied evidence. Public visibility and stigma can materially reduce participation in income-support systems. Governance attention is expanding toward provenance, attestation, and life-cycle assurance for AI-related infrastructure. Device-free boundaries offer a localized form of digital resistance, and an early invasive neural-interface trial provides a limited medical precursor to richer human-computer communication. The evidence does not establish broad automation-caused reductions in working hours, a completed transition from survival work toward care or contribution, widespread analog adoption, safe high-bandwidth neural integration, or sustained off-world settlement. Four conservative canon implications are recommended for human review; the assumption registry and canon remain unchanged.

### AI-generated applications are narrowing entry-level pathways and increasing the value of human referrals

- Event date: 2026-09-02
- Sources: `S1`
- Observed fact: The World Economic Forum reported on September 2, 2026 that generative AI is making job applications easier to produce but harder to distinguish. The report describes a possible shift toward referrals and other social signals, while entry-level roles become more demanding. It cites evidence that AI-exposed entry-level roles in the United States are seven times more likely to require skills traditionally associated with more senior positions, and that 43% of surveyed CEOs planned to deprioritize junior hiring in the following year.
- Significance: This is a measurable social adaptation to workplace automation: when standardized applications become abundant and cheap, employers may place greater weight on reputation, referrals, networks, and demonstrated human judgment. It supports a near-term risk that automation does not simply eliminate tasks but also weakens traditional career-entry systems, potentially increasing the importance of social capital and pre-existing networks. Limitations: The World Economic Forum article is a synthesis of external surveys and reporting rather than a single primary labor-market dataset. The cited statistics cover different populations, countries, and methodologies; they should not be treated as one unified causal estimate. The evidence concerns hiring and career access, not a demonstrated long-term shift from survival work toward meaning, care, or emotional development.

### A large-sample experimental study finds that public visibility sharply reduces take-up of income assistance

- Event date: 2026-08
- Sources: `S4`
- Observed fact: A Journal of Public Economics study in volume 260, published in August 2026, experimentally examined how social observability affects applications for transfers among low-income participants. The study reports that nearly all participants applied when applications remained private, while public applications reduced take-up by 31 percentage points; peer-funded transfers reduced take-up by an additional 10 percentage points.
- Significance: The finding indicates that social meaning, stigma, and reputational exposure can materially affect whether people use economic-support systems, even when assistance is available. This matters for future abundance or basic-income systems: the design of visibility, privacy, and social labeling may influence participation as much as payment levels do. It also complicates any assumption that material support automatically translates into social freedom or reduced status anxiety. Limitations: The experiment concerns a low-income population in a laboratory or controlled setting and may not generalize to national transfer systems. The study examines application behavior and take-up, not long-term employment, identity, purpose, or well-being. ### A recruiting clinical trial is testing a 128-electrode cortical communication system for people with locked-in syndrome

- Event date: 2026-09-03
- Sources: `S5`
- Observed fact: ClinicalTrials.gov lists the CortiCom trial sponsored by UMC Utrecht as recruiting. The study plans to implant four electrode grids totaling 128 electrodes on the brain surface of participants with locked-in syndrome or related severe motor-communication impairment. It aims to test stable control of assistive functions and a keyword-based speech brain-computer interface, with estimated primary completion in September 2028 and an estimated enrollment of two participants.
- Significance: This is a concrete, early-stage signal for richer two-way communication between nervous systems and computational systems, but it remains medical and assistive rather than general-purpose human-AI integration. The trial demonstrates progress in channel density, speech-related decoding, and possible home use, while also showing that the near-term social value of neural interfaces is likely to emerge first through restoration of communication and agency for people with severe disabilities. Limitations: The trial has an estimated enrollment of only two participants and is not evidence of population-scale neural integration. The intervention is invasive, requires a transcutaneous connector, and involves substantial surgical and long-term safety constraints. The record describes intended outcomes and study design, not successful results. The trial targets locked-in syndrome and should not be generalized to healthy users or emotional-information exchange.
