### U.S. frontier-AI incident governance remains a congressional request rather than a binding reporting regime

- Event date: 2026-10-28
- Sources: `S1`, `S9`
- Observed fact: On October 28, 2026, House Democratic committee leaders with jurisdiction over AI asked five frontier AI companies to provide information about model misalignment and safety incidents, including ongoing reporting to Congress within 48 hours of detecting an incident. - Significance: This is a concrete move toward institutionalizing AI incident disclosure and audit trails rather than relying solely on voluntary company transparency, but it is also counterevidence against the stronger version of PS-AI-003. ### C2PA provenance adoption is presented as accelerating, but provenance does not establish truth and creates anonymity and safety tradeoffs

- Event date: 2026-10-28
- Sources: `S2`, `S3`, `S13`
- Observed fact: At NIST’s Workshop on Privacy-Enhancing Cryptography on October 28, 2026, a Microsoft Research presentation stated that the Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity specification had been adopted by technology providers including Microsoft, Google, Adobe, Meta, and OpenAI; camera manufacturers including Sony, Canon, and Leica; and news organizations including BBC, CBC/Radio-Canada, and AFP. The presentation said major deployments began in 2025 and were accelerating in 2026. It also identified a governance limitation: creators in conflict zones, protests, or corruption reporting may avoid certified media because provenance can expose them to retaliation. The presentation proposed blind signatures and zero-knowledge proofs to hide signer identity because ordinary provenance systems can expose creators. Independent technical analysis of C2PA has argued that the specification provides useful origin information but does not by itself prove that content is true, that the signer is trustworthy, or that all relevant transformations are recorded.
- Significance: This is direct evidence that provenance infrastructure is moving from specification toward multi-sector deployment, but it is not an independently audited deployment census and does not establish population-scale coverage, user comprehension, or trust effects. Provenance can improve inspectability while still failing on truth, context, signer incentives, and coverage. Its privacy tradeoffs are especially important for whistleblowers, protestors, journalists, and people in conflict zones.
