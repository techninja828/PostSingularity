### Grid queues and transmission construction remain a binding constraint on clean-generation deployment

- Event date: 2026-08-08
- Sources: `S5`
- Observed fact: The International Energy Agency reported in 2026 that more than 2,500 GW of renewable, large-load, and storage projects were stalled in grid-connection queues worldwide. - Significance: This directly narrows the claim that cheap generation alone produces abundant usable energy. Limitations: The IEA figures are global aggregates and do not establish that every region faces the same queue or construction conditions. ### Four-hour storage does not solve longer-duration reliability and its capacity value can decline as deployment grows

- Event date: 2026-08-08
- Sources: `S8`, `S9`
- Observed fact: The U.S. Energy Information Administration’s storage analysis explains that battery capacity credit depends on how much energy remains available during the system’s net-peak hours. As more four-hour batteries are added, the peak can become longer in duration, causing the ability of each additional battery to cover the full peak to decrease. EIA also modeled cases in which energy-only revenues were insufficient to cover the investment cost of new standalone battery storage.
- Significance: This is a technical constraint on the idea that rapidly falling battery prices automatically produce firm clean power. Batteries can provide fast response, arbitrage, and short-duration capacity while still requiring additional resources for prolonged heat events, low-renewable periods, seasonal shifts, or multi-day outages. Storage economics also depend on market structures that compensate capacity and reliability services, not merely on low cell prices. Limitations: The cited EIA modeling uses simplifying assumptions, including a four-hour maximum discharge duration, and is not a forecast of every storage technology or market. The analysis predates the priority window and does not capture all cost declines or new long-duration technologies developed after publication. The result concerns standalone storage economics and may differ for co-located solar-plus-storage projects, capacity contracts, or vertically integrated utilities.

Limitations: The cited incidents occurred before the priority window and are used as continuing safety evidence rather than as new August 2026 incidents. The EPA reports no public-health risk from the monitored Moss Landing event after the response; the evidence therefore supports a deployment and emergency-management constraint, not a claim that battery storage is generally unsafe. Fire risk varies by chemistry, enclosure design, monitoring, spacing, suppression systems, and operating conditions.
