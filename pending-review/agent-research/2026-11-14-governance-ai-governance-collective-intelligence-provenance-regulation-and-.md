## Research Question

AI governance, collective intelligence, provenance, regulation, and public legitimacy

## Executive Summary

The evidence shows institutional movement toward AI governance through political deliberation, certification proposals, incident reporting, evaluation, provenance, and cross-border coordination. It does not show that these mechanisms are broadly adopted, binding, independently assured, or effective. Provenance remains vulnerable to non-adoption and can establish traceability without establishing truth; evaluations remain too uneven for confident high-stakes decisions; incident notification and oversight independence can fail; and AI-assisted public input remains design-dependent without demonstrated formal legitimacy. PS-AI-003 is therefore mixed, PS-GOV-001 and PS-NEURO-001/002 remain insufficiently or only partially evidenced, and PS-SPACE-001 is unassessed. Human review should consider four conservative canon qualifications while leaving the assumption registry and canon unchanged.

### Current AI evaluation evidence is too imperfect to support confident governance decisions

- Event date: 2026-11-10
- Sources: `S13`
- Observed fact: A November 2026 analysis of California’s frontier-AI implementation experience reported that current safety and alignment evaluations often cannot provide information with sufficient confidence and precision for governance decisions. The report cited benchmark-quality problems, including an estimate that approximately 30 percent of questions in SWE-Bench Pro were unusable and evidence that common benchmarks can underestimate physics capabilities by up to 30 percentage points. It also noted that Illinois’s mandated audit requirement will not begin until 2028, leaving its effectiveness untested during the current period.
- Significance: This directly narrows optimistic claims about auditability and evidence-based regulation. A formal audit regime cannot reliably assure safety if the underlying evaluations are noisy, incomplete, or poorly matched to real deployment conditions. The delay before mandatory audits become operational also means that governance systems may be legislated before independent assurance capacity, benchmark validity, and auditor expertise have been demonstrated at scale.

### Provenance systems remain vulnerable to non-adoption, false reassurance, and confusion between origin and truth

- Event date: 2026-11-09
- Sources: `S14`, `S15`
- Observed fact: An Internet Governance Forum report on the AI governance implementation gap stated that C2PA provenance adoption has remained largely voluntary and that available tests showed adoption was mostly absent. It also identified the risk that users may interpret the absence of a provenance credential as evidence that content is authentic, creating an implied-truth effect. The report emphasized that provenance records describe the history of a file but do not by themselves establish that the depicted event or claim is true.
- Significance: This is a direct falsifier candidate for PS-AI-003’s expectation that provenance systems will gain broad social adoption as AI influence rises. Cryptographic provenance can improve traceability when preserved, but it does not solve authenticity, truth, or coverage problems when platforms strip metadata, creators do not sign content, or users misunderstand what a credential proves. The result is a risk that provenance infrastructure could increase confidence without reliably increasing correctness.
