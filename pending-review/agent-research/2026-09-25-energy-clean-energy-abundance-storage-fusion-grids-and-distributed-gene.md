The recommended canon action is a medium-priority revision to the Energy Systems Summary that qualifies near-limitless-power framing with transmission, local distribution, storage duration, reserve-margin, dispatchability, and access conditions; all other reviewed canon remains unchanged pending evidence.

## Observed Developments

### IEA estimates grid-enhancing technologies could unlock up to 330 GW without major network reinforcement

- Event date: 2026-09-21
- Sources: `S1`
- Observed fact: On September 21, 2026, the International Energy Agency published Modernising Grids in the Age of Electricity. It estimates that dynamic line and transformer ratings, topology optimisation, and advanced power-flow control could allow up to 330 GW of additional generation, storage, and demand to connect to existing networks without reinforcement. The report estimates that connecting this capacity through conventional network expansion would require approximately USD 100 billion. It also reports that at least 1,700 GW of advanced-stage renewable projects and 600 GW of utility-scale batteries were awaiting connection in 2025. ([iea.org](https://www.iea.org/reports/modernising-grids-in-the-age-of-electricity/executive-summary))
- Significance: This is a material constraint-and-enabler signal for clean-energy abundance. It suggests that transmission and distribution capacity, not only generation cost or storage chemistry, is a major near-term bottleneck. The measured potential for 330 GW of additional connections indicates that software, sensing, and power-flow technologies may accelerate deployment faster than building entirely new corridors, while still leaving substantial physical expansion necessary.

### DOE selects 31 transmission projects expected to make more than 23 GW available

- Event date: 2026-09-24
- Sources: `S2`
- Observed fact: On September 24, 2026, the U.S. Department of Energy announced intended support for 31 grid-improvement projects across 26 states through the SPARK initiative. The projects represent USD 5.25 billion in total investment, including USD 1.9 billion in federal funding and USD 3.35 billion in recipient cost share. DOE says the projects will reconductor or rebuild more than 1,500 miles of transmission lines, deploy grid-enhancing technologies across nearly 21,000 miles, and make more than 23 GW of additional electricity capacity available. ([energy.gov](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-department-announces-speed-power-investments-across-26-states-lower-electricity))
- Significance: This is a concrete deployment and funding signal rather than a purely theoretical estimate. It indicates that reconductoring and grid-enhancing technologies are moving into multi-state infrastructure programs intended to relieve congestion and increase transfer capability without relying exclusively on greenfield transmission corridors.

### U.S. pumped-storage hydropower remains the dominant utility-scale storage capacity source in DOE’s 2026 market report

- Event date: 2026-09-18
- Sources: `S3`
- Observed fact: On September 18, 2026, DOE released the 2026 U.S. Hydropower Market Report. The report states that the U.S. had 41 pumped-storage hydropower plants with 22.23 GW of generating capacity and 553 GWh of storage capacity at the time of the report, representing 82.1% of all U.S. utility-scale energy-storage capacity. The report also identified 74 pumped-storage projects in development, including 68 projects totaling 59 GW pursuing FERC permitting. ([energy.gov](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/water/articles/energy-department-releases-2026-us-hydropower-market-report-showing-steady))
- Significance: This is a significant counterweight to battery-centered narratives about storage. It shows that long-duration, reservoir-based storage remains a large installed asset and that the development pipeline is substantial. Pumped storage can provide multi-hour balancing, capacity, and system flexibility needed as variable generation and electrified demand expand.

### Fusion cooperation expands from scientific research toward engineering coordination

- Event date: 2026-09-21
- Sources: `S4`
- Observed fact: On September 21, 2026, ITER announced that the ITER Organization and China Fusion Energy Co., Ltd. signed a memorandum of understanding covering fusion engineering technologies, collaborative research, and international scientific exchange. The agreement followed a September 15 side event at the International Atomic Energy Agency’s 70th General Conference focused on the engineering path from fusion research toward practical energy applications. ([iter.org](https://www.iter.org/node/20687/iter-and-china-strengthen-cooperation-fusion))
- Significance: The development is relevant to fusion commercialization because it emphasizes shared engineering challenges rather than only plasma-performance milestones. It supports the assessment that fusion progress is increasingly dependent on international supply chains, materials, component engineering, and coordinated demonstration pathways.

### DOE benchmarks quantum and hybrid computing for storage and microgrid planning instead of claiming superiority

- Event date: 2026-09-24
- Sources: `S5`
- Observed fact: On September 24, 2026, DOE announced the winning teams in an energy-infrastructure challenge that tested quantum and hybrid computing for deciding where to deploy energy storage and microgrids and at what capacity. Teams executed their approaches on quantum hardware and simulation environments and compared the results with classical baselines. DOE explicitly described the effort as a benchmark intended to measure present capabilities and limitations rather than assume quantum advantage. ([energy.gov](https://www.energy.gov/technologycommercialization/articles/doe-announces-winning-teams-quantum-grid-planning-competition))
- Significance: This is an early but useful signal for distributed-generation planning. Storage and microgrid siting become harder as data centers, advanced manufacturing, rooftop generation, and other distributed resources increase system complexity. The decision to require classical baselines is evidence of a more skeptical evaluation posture and creates reference points for future claims of computational advantage.

### Emergency reliability orders expose near-term grid stress despite ongoing clean-energy and storage expansion

- Event date: 2026-09-17 to 2026-09-20
- Sources: `S6`, `S7`
- Observed fact: During the priority window, DOE issued emergency actions affecting multiple U.S. regions. On September 17, 2026, DOE directed PJM to dispatch specified resources and authorized backup generation during stressed Mid-Atlantic conditions; the order remained effective through September 18. On September 18, DOE issued Order 202-26-47 directing CenterPoint Energy and MISO to keep Unit 2 of the F.B. Culley Generating Station available from September 20 through December 18. DOE cited elevated demand, generation outages, planned transmission outages, exports, and reliability concerns. ([energy.gov](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-secretary-secures-mid-atlantic-grid-due-anticipated-stressed-system-conditions))
- Significance: These orders are a direct falsifier pressure against any simple assumption that declining renewable costs automatically produce abundance or reliability. They indicate that, in at least some regions, system operators still require emergency access to conventional generation and backup resources during tight conditions. The signal strengthens the case that storage duration, transmission, reserve margins, dispatchability, and seasonal adequacy remain material bottlenecks.
