## Executive Summary

The audited evidence supports a qualified, not transformational, reading of automation and AI adoption during 2026-09-29 through 2026-10-06. Labor evidence shows that local skills, retraining, labor demand, social protection, and collective representation mediate outcomes; it does not show reduced aggregate working hours, broad abundance, or a shift from survival work toward meaning, care, creativity, or emotional development. AI-related adaptation appears to include skill sorting and contested distribution rather than automatic liberation from compulsory work. Governance institutions are demanding provenance, accountability, human oversight, and inclusion, but regulatory implementation is staged, uneven, and slower than deployment. Political hearings, privacy concerns, and digital non-use demonstrate resistance to seamless integration, although no evidence establishes population-wide analog revival or durable separation from digital systems. Neural-interface research improves decoding stability in mice but remains far from human, bidirectional, affective, or socially integrated neural communication. The repository mapper recommends conservative, human-reviewed qualifications to the timeline, Trust Fabrics, and Neural Links; no canon or assumption registry has been modified.

- Proposed verdict: **mixed**
- Confidence: **medium**
- Sources: `S11`, `S12`, `S13`, `S14`
- Evidence: Evidence supports visible resistance to pervasive AI integration through political contestation, privacy concerns, and socially meaningful digital non-use. Resistance may appear more through regulation, hearings, consent demands, and selective disconnection than through population-wide analog revival.
- PostSingularity implication: Even under pervasive AI and neural technology, a post-singularity society may preserve tactile, private, and low-technology practices as deliberate boundaries around agency and meaning. ### PS-AI-003: AI influence drives stronger provenance and audit systems

- Proposed verdict: **strengthened**
- Confidence: **medium**

- Evidence: Institutional evidence shows AI governance being framed around trust, accountability, authoritative sources, human oversight, and inclusion. These systems should be expected to develop unevenly, with significant delays, jurisdictional variation, and gaps between formal requirements and effective enforcement.
- PostSingularity implication: A post-singularity society would likely treat inspectable provenance, verification rituals, and accountability mechanisms as core infrastructure for legitimate AI-mediated decisions.
