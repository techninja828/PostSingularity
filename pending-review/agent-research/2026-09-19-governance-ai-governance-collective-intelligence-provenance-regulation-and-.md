### Provenance watermarking can create a detector shortcut instead of reliable verification

- Event date: 2026-06-22
- Sources: `S10`
- Observed fact: A 2026 research paper examined audio provenance watermarking and reported that when synthetic speech is watermarked and human speech is not, detectors trained alongside the watermark can learn a spurious shortcut: treating the watermark itself as the main signal for identifying fake audio. The paper argues that this can undermine the intended forensic function of provenance marking and make detector performance depend on artifacts of the watermark rather than generalizable evidence of synthetic generation.
- Significance: This is a direct falsifier for the assumption that wider provenance adoption necessarily yields trustworthy verification. A provenance marker can become a brittle or misleading signal, especially when detection systems are trained on narrow distributions. Adoption of watermarking may therefore increase the appearance of auditability without guaranteeing robust attribution, especially across changing generators and adversarial conditions.
