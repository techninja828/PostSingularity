## Vignette

Arin keeps his mapping drones on a short leash even when the water is clear.

Not because the machines fail. Because a long leash starts making decisions that look like help. Wide search patterns. Optimistic silt angles. A cheerful tug toward a prettier corridor that is not the waypoint he sang.

On a short leash the drones stay in his peripheral judgment: two bodies, one intent. He can mute the neural link to mapping-only and still feel when a unit wants to wander. Etiquette among oceanic runners is simple and mostly unspoken: if your swarm is teaching you the route, you are already late.

He names it to apprentices without poetry. “Short leash. You steer. They listen. Long leash is how nurseries get interesting visitors they did not invite.”

Then he checks the clips one more time, the way someone checks a knot they already trust.
